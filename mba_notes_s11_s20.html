Streisand Effect S11
📓 Your Notes

Streisand Effect — Social phenomenon where an attempt to hide, remove or censor a piece of information has the unintended consequence of publicizing it more widely, usually facilitated by the internet.

📖 Deep Dive

Patagonia's "Don't Buy This Jacket" (Black Friday 2011) is the textbook case. A campaign designed to raise awareness about overconsumption caused a 30% increase in sales. Indian parallel: Zerodha's CEO openly discourages over-trading — yet this builds immense trust, making them India's largest broker with $0 ad spend.

Patagonia's Purpose Model S11
📓 Your Notes

Patagonia aim → create long lasting sustainable clothing. They donated 1% of yearly profit to environmental care.

📖 Deep Dive

In 2022, Yvon Chouinard transferred Patagonia (~$3B) to the Holdfast Collective — all profits go to nature. Structure split:

🍳 The Kitchen (Purpose Trust)
100% Voting Rights. Decides mission & strategy. Paid $17.5M gift tax.
💰 The Register (Holdfast Collective)
98% Economic Rights. Receives dividends. Zero Votes. $180M paid to Earth (2022–2025).

Indian parallel: Tata Group — ~66% of Tata Sons held by philanthropic trusts, similar stewardship model.

Economic Value Created (V – C) S12
📓 Your Notes
V − C = Economic Value Created
📖 Deep Dive

V = Max Willingness to Pay (WTP)  |  C = Total cost to produce  |  P = Actual price charged.

Differentiation
Raise V above competitors → charge premium P.
Cost Leadership
Deliver same V at lower C → offer lower price.

Fabindia (High C, High V via heritage) vs Zudio (Low C, Medium V via scale).

Schumpeter's Creative Destruction S13
📓 Your Notes

Blockbuster replaced by Netflix.

Schumpeter's theory — How superior formats ruthlessly replace the old.

📖 Deep Dive

"Stabilized capitalism is a contradiction in terms." The 'Perennial Gale' incessantly destroys old industries and creates new ones. Blockbuster's 9,000 stores became liabilities when streaming arrived — assets of the old era become anchors in the new.

🇮🇳 Indian parallel: PCO booths evaporated overnight with Jio's cheap data. Kirana stores now face the same gale from e-commerce.

4 I's of Innovation S13
📓 Your Notes

4 I's → Idea, Invention, Innovation, Imitation

💡 Idea
Abstract concept from basic research. Einstein's light theory → wireless comm. ISRO Mangalyaan concept.
🔬 Invention
Transforms idea into a new product/process. Patents vs. Trade Secrets. Coca-Cola recipe (secret) vs. Intel 4004 (patent).
🚀 Innovation
Commercialization of invention. Google's PageRank = invention; Google Ads = innovation. Netflix DVD → Streaming.
🔁 Imitation
Competitors copy success → innovator must find the next gale. Streaming wars after Netflix. Quick-commerce copies after Blinkit.
Key Insight: Innovation is not invention. It is the commercialization of an invention. Paytm didn't invent QR codes — it commercialized them for India's mass market.

Flywheel Effect S14
📓 Your Notes

Flywheel effect → Series of small wins build upon each other over time, creating self-reinforcing momentum that eventually becomes difficult to stop.

📖 Deep Dive — Amazon Flywheel
Lower Prices → More Traffic → More Sellers → Better Selection → Better Experience → Lower Cost Structure → Lower Prices

By 2025, Amazon earns $716.9B revenue. AWS alone runs at $132B annualized — funded by the flywheel effect of retail scale. Generative AI (Rufus assistant, 250M+ users) now powers the next loop.

Business vs Corporate Strategy
📖 Deep Dive
Business Strategy
"How to compete?" — Cost leadership vs. Differentiation.
Corporate Strategy
"Where to compete?" — 3 dimensions: Value Chain, Products/Services, Geography.
Make vs Buy (Transaction Cost Economics)
📖 Deep Dive

Nobel Laureate Ronald Coase's key insight: when Cin-house < Cmarket, vertically integrate (Make).

Make (Firm)Buy (Market)
AdvantagesCommand & control, deep knowledge, qualityHigh-powered incentives, flexibility, competition
DisadvantagesBureaucracy, Principal-Agent problemsSearch costs, opportunism, info asymmetry
India ExampleReliance integrates backward (oil for textiles)Maruti uses vast vendor ecosystem in Gurugram
Types of Diversification
📖 Deep Dive
Related Diversification
Links exist between SBUs. Economies of scope. Global: Disney (Movies+Parks+Merchandise). India: ITC (FMCG+Hotels+Agri).
Unrelated (Conglomerate)
Few links. Often succeeds in emerging markets to fill voids. Global: Berkshire Hathaway. India: Tata Sons (Steel+Salt+Software).

Diversification Discount: Related diversification yields highest performance. Extreme conglomeration often destroys value due to coordination complexity.

Build, Borrow, Buy Framework S15
📓 Your Notes

Build, Borrow, Buy Framework

🏗 BUILD (Internal)
Use when internal resources are highly relevant. High control, slow. India: Zomato (own delivery fleet). Global: Meta Threads (vs buying Twitter).
🤝 BORROW (Alliance)
Use when resources can be traded/partnered. Faster than build, cheaper than buy. India: Ola + PhonePe. Global: Lyft + GM + Waymo.
🛒 BUY (Acquisition)
When you need extreme closeness and can integrate. Fastest but riskiest. India: Walmart buys Flipkart ($16B). Global: Disney buys Pixar ($7.4B).
📖 Deep Dive — Decision Flow

Ask in sequence: (1) Are internal resources relevant? → BUILD. (2) Are resources tradable? → BORROW via contract. (3) Need closeness? → BORROW via JV. (4) Can you integrate? → BUY. If none apply → Rethink strategy.

⚠ Microsoft + Nokia: $9.4B acquisition failed. Integration was impossible. Nokia's culture clashed. Nearly entire deal written off. Lesson: BUY only when integration is genuinely feasible.

Porter's Diamond — National Competitive Advantage S16
📓 Your Notes

Porter's Diamond: Competitive Intensity (in focal industry) ↔ Factor Conditions ↔ Demand Conditions ↔ Related & Supporting Industries

Competitive Intensity in Focal Industry
Factor Conditions
Skilled labor, infrastructure. India: TCS/Infosys leverage English-speaking engineers. Silicon Valley: engineering talent density.
Demand Conditions
Sophisticated local customers force innovation. Finland's Nokia: remote populations needed reliable comms. India: UPI born from demonetization demand.
Related & Supporting Industries
Strong supplier clusters. Toyota's network of world-class suppliers. Chennai auto cluster: Hyundai, BMW, Ford.
Competitive Intensity
Fierce domestic rivalry forces efficiency. German Autobahn → superior auto engineering. India: Jio price wars → world's cheapest mobile data.
📖 Deep Dive

Natural resources ≠ competitive advantage. Venezuela (resource-rich) has no leading global firms. Japan and Singapore (resource-poor) have dominant global industries because constraint breeds creativity.

Evolution of Org Structure S17
📓 Your Notes

Evolution of org. structure: Simple → Functional → Multidivisional (M-Form) → Matrix

Simple
Startup. Founder decides all. Flat, generalist. Fast but doesn't scale. Example: Kirana store, early Facebook.
Functional
Departments by function (R&D, Marketing, Finance). Good for cost leadership. Example: Walmart, Maruti.
Multidivisional (M-Form)
SBUs with own P&L. Good for diversification. Example: Tata Group, Google → Alphabet (2015).
Matrix
Dual reporting (function + geography). Good for transnational firms. Complex — "two bosses." Example: Unilever.
Organizational Culture (The Culture Onion) S17
📓 Your Notes

The culture union → Artifacts, Norms, Values

Artifacts (Outer)
Visible, tangible. Easy to observe, hard to decipher. Zappos: pays employees $2K to quit. Google: free food, scooters. Indian startups: founders in open offices.
Norms (Middle)
Unwritten behavioral rules. "Always consult before deciding" (W.L. Gore). "Don't leave until client is satisfied" (consulting).
Values & Beliefs (Core)
Underlying reasons for behavior. Tata: "Integrity & Stewardship" explains no mass layoffs. Zappos: "Deliver Wow" explains the $2K offer.

Agency Theory S18
📓 Your Notes

Agency Theory — Arises when goals of the owners & the shareholders/people running the industry do not align.

📖 Deep Dive
Adverse Selection (Before)
Agent hides info to secure deal. Example: Nikola Motors — CEO rolled truck down hill to fake a demo. India: TCS Recruitment Scam (bribes for unqualified hires).
Moral Hazard (After)
Agent takes undue risks when they don't bear the cost. Example: 2008 banks took subprime risks knowing govts would bail them out. India: PNB & Nirav Modi — bank officials issued unauthorized guarantees.
Information Asymmetry & Market for Lemons S18-20
📓 Your Notes

Information asymmetry → one party knows more than the other. Referenced in your notes as a key concept.

📖 Deep Dive — Akerlof's Lemons (Nobel Prize, 1970)
🍑 Peach (Good car)
Worth $10K. Seller knows quality. Won't sell at $7K average price → exits market.
🍋 Lemon (Bad car)
Worth $4K. Gets $7K from uninformed buyer → profit. Stays in market. Result: only lemons remain.
Fix (Signaling): Peach sellers offer warranties → proves quality. Lemon sellers won't — it would cost them too much when the car breaks.

India example: Pre-RERA real estate market — builders held vast private information over buyers. Post-RERA: mandated disclosures reduce asymmetry.
