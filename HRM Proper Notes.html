Section I

Performance Management System (PMS)

Defining, measuring, motivating, and developing the employee's goal-oriented performance on a continuing basis.

The PMS Process

  1. Set goals together (Start of the year)
  2. Tracking progress & Mid-year Review
  3. Year-end Appraisal
  4. Rewards & Development

Current Trends

  • Continuous Performance Management System (Regular check-ins & growth conversations).
  • Decoupling development feedback from pay.
  • Agile goal settings (OKRs).
  • Reduced role of strict ratings.
  • Hybrid & Remote context sensitivity.
  • Enhanced Manager capabilities.

Who Does the Appraisal?

  • Immediate Supervisor: Still the heart of most processes; best positioned to observe.
  • 360-Degree Feedback: Includes subordinates, peers, and external stakeholders.
  • Peer Appraisal & Self-Rating.
  • Rating Committees.
  • Note: HR acts as an advisor; the final decision lies with the operating manager.

Appraisal Techniques

1. Graphic Rating Scale: Used to assess generic job dimensions like communication, teamwork, and know-how.

2. Alternation Ranking: Cross out names not known well, then alternately rank the highest (+ve) and lowest (-ve) on the specific performance dimension until all are ranked.

3. Critical Incidents Method: The supervisor keeps a log of positive and negative examples of subordinate's work-related behavior. Discussed every 6 months. Provides concrete examples but doesn't produce relative ratings easily for pay raises.

4. Bell Curve: Used to reward top talent, motivate the majority, and weed out underperformers.

5. BARS (Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scale):

Combines narratives, critical incidents, and quantified scales by anchoring a scale with specific behavioral examples of good/poor performance. Highly accurate and consistent.

Steps to Develop BARS:
  1. Write critical incidents.
  2. Develop performance dimensions.
  3. Reallocate incidents.
  4. Scale the incidents.
  5. Develop a final instrument.

6. MBO (Management by Objectives): Cascading goals from Corporate Strategic Goals → Departmental → Individual Goals → Action Plans. Requires regular progress reviews.

Evaluation Biases

  • Recency Effect: Focusing only on the most recent behavior rather than the entire review period.
  • Halo/Horn Effect: One positive (Halo) or negative (Horn) trait influences the evaluator to rate all other aspects accordingly.
  • Central Tendency Bias: Rating everyone around the middle of the scale to avoid very high or low ratings.
  • Leniency Bias: Rating everyone too positively.
  • Similar-to-me Bias: Favoring employees who share similar characteristics with the evaluator.

AI in PMS

  • Digital Goal Setting: Betterworks, Lattice, 15Five, WorkBoard.
  • Continuous Feedback: Culture Amp, Reflektive.
  • People Analytics: E.g., Microsoft Viva tracks collaboration patterns and burnout risks.
  • Automated Nudges: Reminders for missed 1-on-1s or feedback check-ins.
  • Performance Dashboards: Workday, SuccessFactors.

Conducting the Interview

A successful appraisal interview requires the manager to: Prepare, Plan, act as a Coach, and remain Objective throughout the conversation.

Section II

Training & Development

Onboarding: Employee orientation programs provide new employees the basic background information required to perform their jobs satisfactorily.

The ADDIE Process

Analysis Design Development Implementation Evaluation

1. Training Needs Analysis (TNA)

Globally, 50-70% of training programs fail because they do not address the real issue. We must identify: "Can't do" (requires training) vs. "Won't do" (requires motivation/management).

  • New Employees (Task Analysis): Detailed study of the job to determine specific skills required (Uses JD & JS). Lower levels require more training.
  • Current Employees (Performance Analysis): Verifying performance deficiency and determining if training is the solution (Uses Performance Appraisals).

2. Designing the Program

  • SMART Objectives: Address specific deficiencies (knowledge, skills, attitudes).
  • Motivational Environment: Accommodate trainee abilities, secure top-management support, ensure self-efficacy.
  • Meaningful Learning: Provide a bird's-eye view, familiar examples, organize content logically.
  • Skills Transfer: Make it obvious. Provide practice, let trainees learn at their own pace. Ensure conditions resemble the actual work environment.

3. Implementing the Training

Hands-on / On-the-Job Methods
  • Apprenticeship: Structured combination of classroom & OJT. (High cost, highly effective, best for skilled trades).
  • Informal Learning: Observing peers, daily tasks. (Low cost, moderate effectiveness).
  • Job Instruction Training (JIT): Step-by-step approach. (Moderate cost, high effectiveness).
  • Behavior Modeling: Show right/wrong way, practice, feedback. (High effectiveness, best for soft skills/customer service).
  • Coaching / Job Rotation / Peer Training.
Off-the-Job / Media / Simulations
  • Lectures & Presentations: Best for factual info to large groups. (Low cost, moderate effectiveness).
  • Vestibule Training: Training in a separate area using identical equipment to avoid slowing down production. (High cost, high effectiveness).
  • Simulated Learning: Software mimicking real-life high-stakes situations (e.g., pilots, surgeons). (High cost, very high effectiveness).

Management Development

While training is task-oriented, management development expands leadership abilities. Includes: Executive Coaching (identifying strengths/weaknesses), Corporate Universities, Outside Seminars (NHRDN, AIMA, CII).

4. Training Evaluation

Kirkpatrick Model
  1. Reaction: Initial participant feedback (surveys).
  2. Learning: Info absorbed (tests/assessments).
  3. Behavior: Application on the job (observation).
  4. Results: Business level impact (ROI, metrics).
70:20:10 Framework
  • 70% - Experiential Learning (The "Do")
  • 20% - Social Learning (The "Share")
  • 10% - Formal Learning (The "Learn")
Section III

Compensation & Benefits

Direct Financial Payments: Wages, salaries, incentives, commissions, and bonuses.
Indirect Financial Payments: Financial benefits like insurance, vacations, pensions.

Aligning Total Rewards with Strategy

Total rewards include traditional pay, incentives, benefits, challenging jobs, career development, and recognition programs. The future reward experience is holistic, workforce-centric, and embedded in the culture (moving from just "Rewards" to a "Workforce Value Proposition").

Establishing Pay Rates

  • Market-Based Approach: Benchmarking against market pay. Data sourced from consultancy firms (Hey Group, Aon) rather than Glassdoor.
    • Below Market: Startups, or when labor supply > demand.
    • Above Market: To resist attrition for highly stressful jobs (e.g., Nurses), or when demand > supply.
  • Job Evaluation Method: Assigning weights to skills and competencies (higher weights to cognitive skills vs. physical labor).

Executive Pay

Determined by job complexity, employer's ability to pay, and executive human capital. Basic pay gets less weight; highly focused on variable pay & ESOPs.

  • Short-term Incentives: Annual bonus (eligibility often tied to salary grade/band). Target bonus limits set per position based on actual performance and company fund size.
  • Long-term Incentives: Stock Options (right to purchase shares at a specific price) and Golden Parachutes (payout upon termination/ownership change).

Incentives for Sales

Fixed Salary

Simple to switch territories, but can demotivate high performers.

Commission

Attracts high performers, but they may neglect non-selling duties (customer service).

Combination

Most common approach (e.g., 70% Base, 30% Incentive).

Non-Financial & Recognition Awards

While financials are critical at lower levels, non-financial rewards become crucial differentiators at higher levels.

  • Recognition Programs: Formal programs like "Employee of the Month".
  • Social Recognition: Informal manager-employee exchanges, praise, and approval.
  • Performance Feedback: Qualitative/quantitative info on performance.

Benefits Administration

Key policy issues regarding indirect financial payments:

  • Determining who receives coverage (and if retirees are included).
  • Handling probationary periods.
  • Financing benefits and cost-containment.
  • Communicating options (unemployment, vacation, sick leave, severance, pension, work-life).
Section IV

Diversity Management

In an employment context, diversity is defined as the collective mixture of differences and similarities. Having a strategy to promote D&I helps CEOs navigate global complexities (PwC Report).

Strategic Management of Diversity

True diversity management goes beyond hiring. It requires:

  • Top Leadership commitment
  • Diversity as part of the org's strategy
  • Linking diversity to performance
  • Inclusive recruitment & succession planning
  • Continuous diversity training

Dimensions of Diversity

Primary Dimensions

Inborn differences with a lifelong impact: Race, Physical Ability, Sexual Orientation, Ethnicity, Gender, Age.

Secondary Dimensions

Acquired or changed throughout life: Education, Marital/Parental Status, Work Background, Income, Geography, Military Experience, Religion.

The DEIB Framework (AON Hewitt)

  • Diversity: Bringing together different voices & viewpoints.
  • Equity: Recognizing barriers and ensuring impartial, fair practices.
  • Inclusion: Equal access to opportunities and resources.
  • Belonging: Cultivating a space where individuals can be their authentic selves.

Types of Diversity & Interventions

Generational Diversity

Impacts communication, motivation, and leadership. 50% of workers in Indian orgs consider generational conflict an issue (SHRM).
Intervention: Reverse Mentoring (e.g., Max Life Insurance where Gen Z/Millennials mentor CXOs on digital tools and social media).

Gender Diversity

India has a massive working female population, yet women remain underrepresented in the C-Suite. Gender roles still affect perceptions.
Intervention: WoW (Women of Wipro) Mentoring Program. Grooming high-potential middle-management women to create an inclusive leadership team, resulting in increased promotion rates.

Disability

Despite acts, representation can be low due to perceived concerns over safety, cost, and social skills. However, inclusive hiring increases loyalty and public image.
Intervention: Lemon Tree Hotels. Over 13% of employees are persons with disabilities (hearing/speech impaired, orthopedically handicapped, Down Syndrome, Autistic).

Sexual Orientation

40% of the LGBT workforce report facing harassment. Companies are adopting same-sex partner benefits, gender-neutral restrooms, and health insurance covering gender-confirmation.
Intervention: Google's "Gayglers". An employee resource group offering immense support, helping attract top talent.

Stages of Diversity Awareness (M. Bennett)

Denial → Defense → Minimizing Differences → Acceptance → Adaptation → Integration

Section V

Employee Relations & Safety

The process of acquiring, training, appraising, and compensating employees, and attending to their labor relations, health, safety, and fairness concerns.

Workplace Unfairness & Justice

Unfair treatment reduces morale, increases stress, and impacts performance. Employees of abusive supervisors are highly likely to quit.

Why Treat Employees Fairly? Perceptions of fairness (Distributive and Procedural Justice) strongly correlate with enhanced employee commitment, satisfaction, and organizational citizenship behaviors. Climate surveys help assess these relations.

Trade Unions

People unionize to protect themselves from management's whims, feeling unity is the only way to get a fair share. They mainly organize around pay and working conditions.

Union workers generally receive significantly better benefits (paid leave, insurance) and are less impacted by downsizings since they aren't entirely "at will."

Visakha Guidelines & Sexual Harassment

Guidelines laid down by the Supreme Court of India. It is the employer's duty to prevent, deter, and provide procedures for resolving acts of sexual harassment.

Visual: Ogling, Staring, Posters
Verbal: Lewd comments, Whistling, Requests for dates, Dirty jokes
Written: Love letters, Obscene emails/poems
Physical/Power: Touching, Patting, Threatening job loss, Force

Costs of Harassment

  • To Employee: Emotional distress, loss of self-esteem, illness (ulcers, stress), absenteeism, loss of job/career disruption.
  • To Employer: Poor morale, low productivity, turnover, litigation costs, damaged public image.

Responsibilities

  • Manager: Acknowledge legal liability, lead by example, respond promptly/fairly, understand why women remain silent, create a no-retribution culture.
  • Employee: Ask "Do I consent? Does it violate my dignity?", read policies, promptly report incidents.

* Behavior unwanted by the recipient may be harassment regardless of intent.

Workplace Health Hazards & Safety

Health Issues

  • Chemicals & Industrial Hygiene (e.g., Asbestos).
  • Infectious Diseases.
  • Alcoholism & Substance Abuse.
  • Stress, Burnout, and Depression.

Ergonomics & Wellness

  • Solving computer-related ergonomic problems.
  • Repetitive motion disorders.
  • Workplace smoking policies.
  • Comprehensive Wellness Programs.

Occupational Security

  • Crime prevention & Security measures.
  • Employee screening & supervisory training.
  • Preventing violence toward women.
  • Managing Employee Privacy and breaches.
Section VI

International HRM (IHRM)

IHRM involves the concepts and techniques employers use to manage the HR challenges of their international operations. It focuses on three main topics:

  1. Managing human resources in global companies (selecting, training, compensating).
  2. Managing expatriate employees (those sent abroad).
  3. Comparing HRM practices in different countries.

International Staffing Policies

Ethnocentric Approach

Hires Parent Country Nationals (PCNs). Citizens of the country where the MNC is headquartered. Used in MNCs with international strategic orientation.

Polycentric Approach

Hires Host Country Nationals (HCNs). Lowers recruiting/training costs and reduces adjustment/communication problems.

Geocentric Approach

Hires Third Country Nationals (TCNs). Recruits globally based on skill, irrespective of nationality (transnational orientation).

Selecting International Managers

Firms must avoid early expatriate returns. Success depends heavily on:

  • Screening, testing, and being realistic.
  • Fluency in local language.
  • Managing family pressures (e.g., pre/school age children).
  • Legal issues and sending women abroad.

Case Study: Brazil Subsidiary (Hofstede)

When deciding who leads a Brazilian subsidiary (Indian Expat, Local Brazilian, or Global Exec), one must consider Brazilian culture:

  • Relationship-Oriented Culture
  • High Power Distance & Collectivist Tendencies
  • High-Context Communication Style
  • Emotional Expressiveness & Flexibility toward time.

Cross-Cultural Training (CCT)

Crucial for expats to reduce stress, boost self-awareness, and provide coping strategies. Training should cover local history, politics, business norms, cultural values, and why moving is difficult. Strategic HR planning must focus on global competencies.

Compensation & Performance

Balance Sheet Approach: Equalizes purchasing power across countries so the expat maintains their home standard of living. Includes foreign service premiums, hardship allowances, and mobility premiums.

Appraising Expats: Adapt criteria to the local job/situation, weight the evaluation toward the on-site manager, and use a same-country former expat for advice.

Repatriation & Security

Reverse Culture Shock: 40% to 60% of expats quit within 3 years of returning home. Issues include dissatisfaction with the home standard of living, unutilized international KSA (Knowledge, Skills, Abilities), and co-workers uninterested in their experiences.

Labor Relations & Safety: Requires taking protective measures regarding global terrorism, safety, and procuring Kidnapping & Ransom (K&R) insurance.
