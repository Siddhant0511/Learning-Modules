Descriptive Statistics

Summarises & describes data you have.

Empirical Rule — Normal Distribution (68–95–99.7)

Z-Score & Chebyshev

Z-score
Z = (X − μ) / σ
Chebyshev's Theorem (any distribution)
At least (1 − 1/k²) of data lies within k SDs of mean
k=2 → ≥75%  |  k=3 → ≥89%
💡
Empirical Rule only applies to normal distributions. Chebyshev's applies to ANY distribution — use it when you can't assume normality.

Discrete Probability Distributions

STATS

Binomial Distribution — SPIN Conditions

S — fixed number of trials (n)
P — constant probability of success (p)
I — independent trials
N — two outcomes (Success / Failure)
Binomial PMF
P(X=x) = C(n,x) × pˣ × (1−p)^(n−x)
E(X) = np  |  Var(X) = np(1−p)

Poisson Distribution

Poisson PMF — events in fixed interval
P(X=x) = (e^−λ × λˣ) / x!    E(X) = Var(X) = λ
💡
Binomial → Poisson approximation: When n is very large and p is very small, Binomial ≈ Poisson with λ = np.

Binomial PMF — n=10, p=0.5

Poisson PMF — λ=4

S6

Sampling Distribution & CLT

STATS

Central Limit Theorem

Regardless of the population's distribution, if n is large enough (n ≥ 30), the sampling distribution of the mean is approximately normal.

Sampling Distribution of X̄
Mean of X̄ = μ (same as population)
Standard Error (SE) = σ / √n
⚠️
CLT applies to means, not individual values. 50 observations from a skewed population are still skewed. The distribution of many sample means is what becomes normal.

Hypothesis Testing — Z-Test

STATS

5-Step Hypothesis Testing

1
State H₀ and H₁ — H₀ is the status quo (=); H₁ is your claim
2
Choose α — typically 0.05 (5% Type I error tolerance)
3
Compute test statistic — Z = (X̄ − μ₀) / (σ/√n)
4
Find p-value or critical value
5
Decide: p < α → Reject H₀  |  |Z| > Z_crit → Reject H₀

t-Tests

STATS

Z vs t — Decision Rule

ConditionUse ZUse t
Population σ known?YesNo (use sample s)
Sample sizeLarge (n≥30)Any size works
DistributionStandard Normalt with df = n−1
In practiceRareAlmost always
One-Sample t Statistic
t = (X̄ − μ₀) / (s / √n)    df = n − 1
Two-Sample t (Pooled — equal variances)
t = (X̄₁ − X̄₂) / √[sp²·(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)]    df = n₁+n₂−2
sp² = [(n₁−1)s₁² + (n₂−1)s₂²] / (n₁+n₂−2)

t-Distribution — Effect of Degrees of Freedom (heavier tails at low df)

ANOVA — Analysis of Variance

STATS

Why not multiple t-tests?

With 3 groups and α=0.05: P(at least one false positive) = 1−(0.95)³ ≈ 14% — far above the intended 5%.

F-Statistic
F = MSB / MSW = Between-group variance / Within-group variance

SST = SSB + SSW
MSB = SSB/(k−1)  |  MSW = SSW/(n−k)
⚠️
ANOVA tells you THAT a difference exists, not WHERE. Significant F → use post-hoc tests (Tukey, Bonferroni) to find which pairs differ.

Chi-Square Test

STATS

Test of Independence

Tests whether two categorical variables are independent or associated.

Chi-Square Statistic
χ² = Σ [(Observed − Expected)² / Expected]

Expected_ij = (Row_i Total × Col_j Total) / Grand Total
df = (r − 1)(c − 1)
⚠️
Requirement: Expected frequency ≥ 5 in every cell. If not, use Fisher's Exact Test. Chi-square is ONLY for categorical data — never for numerical means.

Clustering

ANALYTICS

K-Means Algorithm

1
Choose K (number of clusters)
2
Initialise K centroids randomly
3
Assign each point to nearest centroid (Euclidean distance)
4
Recalculate centroid = mean of all assigned points
5
Repeat 3–4 until convergence (centroids stop moving)
⚠️
Always scale before K-Means. Salary (0–200,000) dominates Age (20–60) in distance calculations. Scale to remove this bias.

Simple Linear Regression

ANALYTICS

The OLS Model

Simple Linear Regression
Ŷ = β₀ + β₁X

β₁ = Σ(Xᵢ−X̄)(Yᵢ−Ȳ) / Σ(Xᵢ−X̄)²  (minimises SSE)
β₀ = Ȳ − β₁X̄
R² — Coefficient of Determination
R² = SSR / SST = 1 − SSE/SST    (0 to 1, higher = better)
SST = SSR + SSE  (Total = Explained + Unexplained)
LINE Assumptions
LinearityX–Y relationship is linear (check scatter plot)
IndependenceObservations are independent
NormalityResiduals are normally distributed (Q-Q plot)
Equal VarianceHomoscedasticity — constant residual spread
⚠️
Correlation ≠ Causation. High R² does NOT mean X causes Y. Always verify business logic independently.

A8

Multiple Linear Regression (MLR)

ANALYTICS

The Model

Multiple Linear Regression
Ŷ = β₀ + β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + … + βₖXₖ

Each βᵢ = change in Y for 1-unit change in Xᵢ, all other variables held constant
Adjusted R²
Adj. R² = 1 − [(1−R²)(n−1) / (n−k−1)]
Penalises for adding useless predictors. Use this to compare models.

VIF — Multicollinearity Thresholds

Multicollinearity

VIF ValueInterpretationAction
VIF = 1No multicollinearityAll good
VIF 1–5Moderate — acceptableMonitor
VIF 5–10HighConsider removing
VIF > 10SevereMust act
⚠️
Dummy variable trap: For k categories, include only k−1 dummies. Including all k creates perfect multicollinearity (they sum to 1 for every row).

🧠 Quick Check — A8

Adding 5 variables increases R² from 0.72→0.74, but Adjusted R² drops from 0.71→0.69. Conclusion?

The 5 new variables don't add real value — model actually degraded
Model improved — R² went up
Adjusted R² is irrelevant; always use plain R²
